Problem 01: Estimating heartbeats over a lifetime

Inspired by textbook Exercise 1.1 · printed p. 20
P-11-A-01 · Formative

Estimate the order of magnitude of the total heartbeats over 60 years, assuming an average heart rate of 70 beats per minute and 365 days per year.

Step-by-step working

  1. Write the assumptions: 70 beats/min; 60 min/h; 24 h/day; 365 days/year; 60 years.
  2. Number of beats = 70 × 60 × 24 × 365 × 60.
  3. = 2,207,520,000 beats ≈ 2.2 × 109 beats.
  4. The order of magnitude is about 109; the actual value depends on heart rate, age and activity.

Answer: ≈ 2.2 × 10⁹ beats; order 10⁹.

This is estimation rather than an exact physiological prediction. P-11-A-01 is formative in the supplied FBISE framework.

Problem 02: Express derived units in SI base units

Based on section 1.2 · printed pp. 10–11
P-11-A-02 · Summative

Starting from F = ma, derive the SI base units of force, work and pressure. Explain why a newton or joule is not itself a base unit.

Step-by-step working

  1. Force: F = ma, so [F] in units = kg × (m s−2) = kg m s−2.
  2. Work: W = Fd, so [W] = (kg m s−2) × m = kg m2 s−2.
  3. Pressure: P = F/A, so [P] = (kg m s−2)/m2 = kg m−1 s−2.
  4. A newton (N) and joule (J) are named derived units, abbreviating combinations of base units.

Answer: Force: kg m s⁻²; work: kg m² s⁻²; pressure: kg m⁻¹ s⁻².

Problem 03: Check dimensional homogeneity

Based on section 1.3.1 · printed p. 12
P-11-A-03 · Summative

For displacement s, initial velocity u, acceleration a and time t, check whether s = ut + ½at² is dimensionally homogeneous, and explain why s = ut + at is not.

Step-by-step working

  1. Displacement: [s] = L.
  2. First term: [ut] = (L T−1) × T = L.
  3. Second term: [½at²] = (L T−2) × T² = L. The factor ½ is dimensionless.
  4. So the first equation is homogeneous. In the incorrect expression, [at] = (L T−2) × T = L T−1, which is not L.

Answer: s = ut + ½at² is homogeneous; s = ut + at is not.

A dimensionally correct equation is not necessarily physically correct; dimensional analysis cannot establish numerical constants.

Problem 04: Percentage uncertainty in a derived expression

Paraphrase of textbook Exercise 1.3 · printed p. 20
P-11-A-06 · Summative

A calculated quantity has the form A = XY²/Z³. Each of X, Y and Z has a relative uncertainty of 1%. Estimate the percentage uncertainty in A using the chapter's simple rules.

Step-by-step working

  1. Identify the powers: X has exponent 1, Y has exponent 2, and Z appears with exponent −3.
  2. For multiplication, division and powers, add absolute power-weighted percentage uncertainties.
  3. ΔA/A × 100% ≈ 1 × 1% + 2 × 1% + 3 × 1% = 6%.
  4. The negative power of Z does not make its uncertainty subtract: uncertainty contributions add in this conservative method.

Answer: Percentage uncertainty in A ≈ 6%.

Problem 05: Dimensions of a drag-force coefficient

Paraphrase of textbook Exercise 1.4 · printed p. 20
P-11-A-03 · Summative

Suppose a resistive force on a sphere is represented by F = c r v, where r is a length and v is speed. Determine the dimensions and SI base units of c.

Step-by-step working

  1. Rearrange: c = F/(rv).
  2. Substitute dimensions: [c] = [M L T−2]/([L][L T−1]).
  3. Simplify: [c] = [M L−1 T−1].
  4. Replace M, L, T with kg, m, s to get the corresponding SI base units.

Answer: [c] = M L⁻¹ T⁻¹; SI base units kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹.

This analysis applies to the stated model F = c r v; it does not prove that model applies to all flow conditions.

Problem 06: Verify the units of fluid pressure

Paraphrase of textbook Exercise 1.5 · printed p. 21
P-11-A-02 & P-11-A-03 · Summative

A liquid pressure model is P = ρgh, where density ρ is measured in kg m⁻³, g in m s⁻² and h in metres. Confirm the SI base units of P.

Step-by-step working

  1. Write each quantity's units: ρ → kg m−3, g → m s−2, h → m.
  2. Multiply: (kg m−3)(m s−2)(m) = kg m−1 s−2.
  3. This is N m−2, the pascal (Pa).
  4. The dimensional result M L−1 T−2 agrees with pressure = force/area.

Answer: P has SI units kg m⁻¹ s⁻² = Pa.

Problem 07: Electrical power and uncertainty

Worked from textbook Exercise 1.8 data · printed p. 21
P-11-A-06 · Summative

A resistor is measured as R = (13 ± 0.5) Ω, and the current is I = (3.0 ± 0.1) A. Compute P = I²R, the percentage uncertainties of R and I, and the estimated absolute uncertainty in P.

Step-by-step working

  1. Nominal power: P = (3.0 A)² × 13 Ω = 117 W.
  2. Relative uncertainty of resistance = 0.5/13 = 0.03846 → 3.85%.
  3. Relative uncertainty of current = 0.1/3.0 = 0.03333 → 3.33%.
  4. For P = I²R, relative uncertainty ≈ 2(0.03333) + 0.03846 = 0.10513 = 10.51%.
  5. Absolute power uncertainty ≈ 117 × 0.10513 = 12.3 W, giving (117 ± 12) W at suitable uncertainty precision.

Answer: P = 117 W; R: 3.85%; I: 3.33%; ΔP ≈ 12.3 W (about 12 W).

Editorial QC: the printed textbook answer hint appears to give 11.7 W. Using the displayed ±0.5 Ω and ±0.1 A values and the chapter's stated addition rule, retaining intermediate precision gives 12.3 W. Do not silently copy the hint; confirm the classroom reporting convention with your teacher.

Problem 08: Area of a circle with an uncertain radius

Adapted from the chapter's worked area example · printed p. 18
P-11-A-06 · Summative

The radius of a circle is r = (2.25 ± 0.01) cm. Calculate its area and estimate the area uncertainty, using π ≈ 3.14159.

Step-by-step working

  1. Nominal area: A = πr² = π(2.25 cm)² = 15.9043 cm².
  2. Relative uncertainty in r = 0.01/2.25 = 0.004444.
  3. For A ∝ r², relative uncertainty in A ≈ 2 × 0.004444 = 0.008889 = 0.889%.
  4. Absolute uncertainty in A ≈ 15.9043 × 0.008889 = 0.1414 cm².
  5. Report with a consistent number of digits.

Answer: A ≈ (15.90 ± 0.14) cm².

Problem 09: Pendulum period from a timed series

Adapted from the measurement-timing example · printed p. 17
P-11-A-06 & P-11-A-07 · Summative

The total measured time for 20 oscillations is (35.2 ± 0.1) s. Find the period and its absolute uncertainty, assuming the oscillation count is exact.

Step-by-step working

  1. Period T = total time / number of oscillations = 35.2/20 = 1.76 s.
  2. With a fixed exact count N = 20, ΔT = Δt/N = 0.1/20 = 0.005 s.
  3. Express the interval clearly without implying that the stopwatch itself has infinite precision.
  4. Note that the true experimental uncertainty can be larger if reaction time or counting errors are significant.

Answer: T = (1.760 ± 0.005) s under the stated assumptions.

Problem 10: Dimensions of Planck's constant

Inspired by textbook short-response Exercise 1.8 · printed p. 20
P-11-A-02 · Summative

A photon has energy E = hf, where f is frequency. Determine the dimensions and SI units of the constant h.

Step-by-step working

  1. Rearrange: h = E/f.
  2. Dimensions of energy: [E] = [M L² T−2].
  3. Dimensions of frequency: [f] = [T−1].
  4. Divide: [h] = [M L² T−2]/[T−1] = [M L² T−1].

Answer: [h] = M L² T⁻¹; SI base units kg m² s⁻¹ (equivalently J s).
