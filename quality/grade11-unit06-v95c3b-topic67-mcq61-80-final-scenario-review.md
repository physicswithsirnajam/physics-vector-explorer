### Core scientific understanding versus textbook enrichments
**Q61/Q62/Q73/Q75/Q79/Q80 — B43 core:** Liquid over the edge of a vessel exhibits **creep** (Q61 B), while very weak viscous damping permits persistent motion (Q62 D), **not** arbitrary creation of energy (Q73 A). No viscosity does **not** mean no force/mass/momentum (Q75 C), persistent vortex circulation is an apt zero-viscosity implication (Q79 D), and ordinary stirring does not automatically make every liquid superfluid (Q80 B). **Q61** asks which concept *explains* a phenomenon, while the key merely *names* creep; the original is best among supplied choices, but teacher should consider a more precise named-phenomenon stem or a separate true explanation item. Original explanation refers to Figure6.16 but the Q61 stem is **self-contained**, so do not falsely call it a missing figure-dependent question.

**Q63/Q64/Q76 — helium benchmarks:** Helium-4 at 2.00K is below its textbook ~2.17K benchmark while 2.50K is above; Q63 D. The original book lists helium-3's illustrative threshold as ~0.0025K, much lower than helium-4's ~2.17K (Q64 D); confusing helium-4's number with helium-3 is Q76 A. Keep actual pressure/phase-dependent qualifications and distinguish numeric/isotope facts from B43 core Understanding.

**Q65–68/Q77 — rotating vessel and quantization:** Printed p106 discusses ideal superfluid remaining stationary while a vessel rotates below a critical angular velocity (Q65 B, Q77 B), first vortex at onset (Q66 B), increasing number of quantized vortices with rising rotation (Q67 B), and discrete vortex circulation strengths (Q68 A). These are source-based scenario answers but require subcritical/geometry/normal-fluid qualifications. Official B43 emphasizes **persistent stirred vortices**, not specialized threshold formulae or derivation of circulation quantization.

**Q69–72/Q78 — advanced applications:** Printed p106 names high-precision **gyroscopes** (Q69 D), tiny theoretically predicted gravitational effects (Q70 A), specialized slowing/trapping of light (Q71 C), and **possible** superfluid neutron matter in neutron stars (Q72 B). These are *attributed original-book context*, not universally proven guarantees for any arbitrary liquid or astrophysical object. Slowing light requires specific ultracold quantum-optical setups; attributing a printed statement does not justify claiming superfluidity alone stops photons. Q78 C correctly distinguishes quantum superconductivity of electrons from liquid-helium superfluidity, but direct core B43 scope remains unapproved.

**Q74 phase-diagram context:** Original answer D is scientifically valid for original printed **Figure6.17** because both **pressure and temperature** are graphed. However the standalone stem only says different *coordinates* without giving the variables or providing a linked diagram. It should explicitly name pressure and temperature or receive a lawful, legible student-visible figure after teacher approval. Diagram interpretation is not among the B43 named core implications.
