Moment of Inertia, Torque & Angular Momentum Lab

Compare rotating objects with different mass distributions, brake a spinning grinding wheel using net torque, and discover angular-momentum conservation when an ice skater draws weighted arms inward.

Physical model: Lab 1 uses a rigid body rotating about a fixed central axis with constant moment of inertia and net external torque. Lab 2 models a central skater inertia and two equal point masses moving radially in/out under negligible external torque. Rotational kinetic energy need not be conserved in Lab 2. Animation speed is deliberately adjusted for display; computed values use unscaled physics inputs. No grades or student data are recorded.

2. Conservation lab — a skater draws in weighted arms

If the net external torque is negligible, angular momentum is constant. The skater changes mass distribution using internal effort, so the moment of inertia and angular speed can change even when total L does not.

Drawing arms in reduces I and raises angular speed while L remains constant.
