## Scientific distinctions to preserve

- **B-23:** compression/tension bending and torsion concern distinct loading modes; make physical orientation unambiguous instead of invoking an unavailable textbook figure.
- **B-24:** Young's modulus uses **matched axial tensile stress and longitudinal strain** within a proportional elastic range; a correct shear/bulk modulus or unit-conversion problem does not automatically meet this *named* Application outcome.
- **B-25:** the original metallic-wire measurement apparatus needs correct original gauge length, diameter-derived area, applied force *increment* and corresponding extension; a bare remembered equation is weaker than a method explanation.
- **B-27:** `W=∫F dx` is area under a graph with **force vertical, extension horizontal**. The slope represents stiffness, not work. Work and recoverable energy are equal in an ideal reversible linear elastic loading path, but not necessarily for plastic/deformational hysteresis.
- **Duplicate-aware sampling:** Topic7.2 Q51/Q52/Q53 share axial modulus ingredients; Topic7.3 Q7/Q65/Q70 all become graph-area work candidates. Keep them separately for practice but avoid clustering nearly identical operations in one short scored test.
