### Scientific qualifications for this batch

**Atomizer and filter pump (Q21–23):** Higher airflow speed at the pickup opening can create a reduced static-pressure region relative to liquid surface or surroundings, so atmospheric/other greater pressure moves perfume/air into the flow. A weaker water-jet filter pump may draw less air. These are model-dependent trends, not a universal speed–pressure relation independent of geometry, pumps, dissipation or external conditions. Q23 is a candidate B44 real-world explanation but close to formative B39 effect if tested only as a qualitative inverse relation.

**Torricelli/free-surface flow (Q24–30):** Ideal `v=sqrt(2gh)` assumes an open large tank, same ambient pressure at surface and jet, negligible surface velocity and energy losses. `v_surface=(A_exit/A_tank)v_exit` from quasi-steady continuity explains the approximation and is candidate **B36**, not automatically summative B40. Density cancellation is valid in the textbook single-fluid ideal model, not universal for pressurized or high-loss outlets. Zero `h` gives zero unforced ideal efflux in the limiting model; real viscous head loss can reduce exit speed and links to distinct B41 Understanding context.

**Venturi and carburetor (Q31–35):** Fixed `Q` leads to speed `v=Q/A`; ideal horizontal Bernoulli then predicts lower throat static pressure. **Q32** pressure difference is the measurement signal, but calculating speed also requires density and area ratio, plus controlled loss assumptions. **Q33** alone is continuity B36. **Q34** at equal elevations cancels `ρgh` on both sides—it does not eliminate gravity. Printed NBF p.104 carburetor crossflow and lower-pressure fuel aspiration support Q35.

**Aerofoil and Magnus (Q36–39):** The simplified p.104 Fig6.14 illustrates low static pressure above/higher below, supporting Q36 B and Q37 B. The figure's longer/shorter path labels should **not** be used to assert that two parcels must arrive simultaneously. Real aerodynamic lift depends on the integrated pressure and shear distribution and deflection of air, not solely a pictorial speed difference. Printed p.105 Fig6.15 describes spin-related asymmetric flow and transverse Magnus force. Q38 D and Q39 B are supported; exact force direction depends on rotation axis and relative wind. These two prepared-source Understanding items are not automatically certified as B44 Application-level exam analysis.

**Two-sheet activity (Q40):** Original printed p.101 classroom demonstration explains sheets moving inward through lower pressure between them (A). This is valid Bernoulli effect **formative B39** context; it must be considered separately before promotion to final-theory B44 Application assessment.
