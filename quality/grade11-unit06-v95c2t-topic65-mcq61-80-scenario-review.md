### Key independent physics reasoning and scope

**Q61/Q66 — a narrower horizontal section:** In constant-density, unbranched, steady flow, `A1v1=A2v2` makes the narrower section faster. For ideal no-loss/no-shaft-work flow along a streamline at the same elevation, Bernoulli then predicts a lower static pressure. Q66 explicitly says ideal; **Q61** says smooth and steady but does **not** explicitly declare inviscid/no-pump model: original D remains the intended classroom answer but requires that qualification before individual board-style use.

**Q62–65 — height and area effects:** At equal speed, rising 4 m reduces static pressure by `ρg×4 m`; descending at equal speed increases pressure. Rising and narrowing both consume available pressure energy; descending and widening both raise pressure under the same ideal, no-leak, constant-density flow model.

**Q67/Q69 — continuity premises:** Unchanged A alone doesn't fix same speed unless steady **incompressible** no-leak flow is established; this is the hidden premise in Q67. Q69 directly asks a steady incompressible continuity result `A1v1Δt=A2v2Δt`, candidate separate B36; **do not mechanically certify either as B40 Application**.

**Q68/Q71 — non-ideal flow limits:** Substantial viscous losses dissipate mechanical energy, and a pump adds shaft work. The unmodified ideal Bernoulli equality cannot simply be applied across either without additional terms. Possible B41 context for viscous behavior remains a **candidate** only; Q71's pump-extension is beyond the simple printed p.99–100 equality.

**Q73 — competing effects:** Widening decreases speed and tends to raise pressure; greater elevation tends to lower it. Without numbers and a declared ideal constant-discharge model, a unique pressure change cannot be given (original B).

**Q76/Q78 — density qualifications:** Q76's 0.8 scaling compares two separate density assumptions under fixed speed readings, **not** a real incompressible fluid changing density along one streamline. **Q78 is under-specified**: the same cross-sectional area does not imply equal speeds for all fluids, because a compressible stream can change density. Original C is correct under **incompressible** lossless same-streamline assumptions and must be clarified before certification.

**Q77:** With water density `ρ≈1000 kg/m³`, `ΔP=½ρ(8²−4²)=24 kPa`; the value is scientifically supported. **Q79** corresponds to the printed pressure-work-to-mechanical-energy principle and is accurate as qualitative understanding. **Q80** correctly identifies constant Bernoulli sum along the same ideal streamline; source cognitive tag Understanding cannot be silently promoted to B40's Application cognitive requirement.
