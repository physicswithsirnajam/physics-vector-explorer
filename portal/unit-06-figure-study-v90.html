Figure 6.2 · Pressure forces on an immersed cylinder

Original explanatory redraw. Depth increases downwards; the bottom pressure force is upwards.

Core relation: F₂ = P₂A. For this idealized horizontal-face case, net upthrust is F₂ − F₁ = ρgV. The original MCQ is now selectable alongside its original educational diagram.

Figure 6.3 · Why geese fly in a V formation

Original simple bird-flight symbols, not a reproduction of the textbook photograph.

Core idea: The textbook associates V formation with reduced drag and energy use. Real flock aerodynamics depends on position, wing motion, and wake interactions; the simplified caption is not a universal guarantee for every bird.

Figure 6.5(c) · Paratrooper's downward speed–time graph

Original graph based on the values described in the textbook text; not a scan or pixel-perfect recreation of Fig. 6.5(c).

Core idea: A horizontal speed–time section means constant speed and zero acceleration, even though the paratrooper is still moving downwards. The textbook model has one steady stage before canopy opening and another at a lower speed afterwards.

Figure 6.6 · Smooth streamline versus turbulent flow

Original side-by-side teaching diagram, following the textbook's (a) streamline and (b) turbulent labels.

Core distinction: Smooth ordered particle paths versus irregular, swirling paths. At a more advanced level, “steady” and “laminar” are not universally interchangeable, and a non-rotating flow need not follow perfectly straight paths.
