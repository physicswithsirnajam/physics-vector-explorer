Teaching guide · classroom and home

For the classroom projector: Predict the range at 30°, 45° and 60°. Run the same-speed launches, then turn on the complementary-angle comparison. Pause at the peak to identify the vertical velocity and acceleration.

  • Compare horizontal launches with textbook Example 3.4 (12 m/s from 24.6 m).
  • Try Example 3.5 (200 m/s at 30°): calculate time, maximum height and range.
  • Pause at the peak: why is vᵧ = 0 but aᵧ = −g?

FBISE alignment: P-11-B-04 (equations of motion), P-11-B-05 (ideal projectile motion) and P-11-B-06 (qualitative effect of air resistance) are summative. The physical model here deliberately neglects air resistance; no numerical drag calculation is implied.

Aim: Investigate ideal projectile motion by varying launch speed, angle and initial height; relate observations to equations and graphs.

Experiment 1 — Same launch and landing height

  1. Set u = 25 m/s, h = 0 m and θ = 30°. Record R, T and maximum height above ground.
  2. Repeat at θ = 45° and θ = 60°. Which gives the maximum range? Which pair of angles gives the same range?
  3. Pause at the highest point. Write vₓ, vᵧ and aᵧ and explain your observations.

Experiment 2 — Horizontal launch

  1. Choose “Horizontal launch” (u = 12 m/s, h = 24.6 m). Find flight time from vertical motion, then calculate range from horizontal motion.
  2. Explain why vₓ stays constant while vᵧ changes during the flight.

Experiment 3 — FBISE reasoning and calculation

  1. For u = 200 m/s, θ = 30°, h = 0 m, calculate time of flight, maximum height and range (use g = 9.8 m/s²).
  2. Explain qualitatively how air resistance changes the motion compared with this ideal simulation.

Reflection: What is independent about horizontal and vertical motion in the ideal model?
