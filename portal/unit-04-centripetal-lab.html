P-11-B-14 · SQUARED-SPEED RELATION

2. Graph the relationship Fc ∝ v²

Horizontal axis: speed from 0 to 30 m/s. Vertical axis: Fc in N. Mass and radius stay at the selected values.

When speed doubles at fixed mass and radius, the required inward force quadruples. Doubling the radius halves the force.

Physics behind the curve

The horizontal coordinate is speed v, but the vertical value follows the square: Fc = (m/r)v². Doubling mass gives twice the inward force. For zero speed, the required centripetal force is zero.

Challenge: compare a turn at 10 m/s and 20 m/s without changing the other sliders. Notice that the force is four times greater.

P-11-B-15 · IDEAL BANKED-ROAD APPLICATION

3. Friction-free banked road: required angle

For a road banked through angle θ to the horizontal, the horizontal component of the normal reaction supplies the inward force, while its vertical component balances weight. This applies only at the ideal design speed, without friction or vertical acceleration.

N cosθ = mg    |    N sinθ = mv²/r    ⇒    tanθ = v²/(rg)

r = mv²/Fc = (856 × 12.0²)/7250 ≈ 17.0 m
