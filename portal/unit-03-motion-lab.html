Grade XI · Unit 03 · Translatory Motion

Interactive Motion Laboratory

Change the conditions of motion and observe how velocity, displacement and projectile trajectories respond. Each model is grounded in the equations and illustrations on NBF Physics XI Unit 3 textbook pages 33–43.

Learning versus examination: deriving equations of motion (P-11-B-03) is formative, while numerical application (P-11-B-04) and projectile analysis (P-11-B-05) are summative under the supplied FBISE Grade XI Assessment Framework. This is an original public self-learning simulation, not an official examination or verified student grade. It does not require sign-in or save activity to your account.

1. Motion along a straight line

Assume constant acceleration along a chosen positive axis. The velocity–time graph’s slope is acceleration, while its signed area gives displacement, not necessarily distance travelled.

Velocity–time: gradient = a; signed area = s

Displacement–time: gradient = v

Textbook equations (uniform a): v = u + at s = ut + ½at² v² = u² + 2as

These hold for signed straight-line motion under constant acceleration, even if the object reverses direction. Do not confuse a signed displacement with total distance.

2. Projectile motion with no air resistance

Launch and land at the same height, with constant downward g = 9.8 m/s². The horizontal velocity remains constant, while the vertical velocity changes with time. Air resistance and cliff launches are outside this first simulator.

The graph rescales to show the full trajectory. Distance units remain metres; drawn x/y scales may differ to make tall or shallow trajectories visible.

Textbook projectile relationships: uₓ = u cos θ; uᵧ = u sin θ x = (u cos θ)t; y = (u sin θ)t − ½gt² vₓ = u cos θ; vᵧ = u sin θ − gt T = 2u sin θ/g; H = u²sin²θ/(2g) R = u²sin(2θ)/g

For equal launch/landing heights without air drag, complementary launch angles give the same range, and 45° maximizes range at fixed speed.
