## Scientific interpretation, caveats and independently checked practice

- **First Law:** Q=ΔU+W_by and, for a complete cycle, ΔU_cycle=0. The Second Law adds constraints on direction and on cyclic heat-to-work conversion; conservation alone does not establish physical feasibility.
- **Kelvin–Planck (Lord Kelvin) statement:** no cyclic heat engine can receive heat from **one reservoir only** and convert the entirety to net work **as its sole effect**. The restriction is **not** a ban on all finite heat-to-work conversions. A normal cyclic engine exchanges heat with hot source and cooler sink.
- Define Q_H and Q_C as **positive magnitudes**, not two signed inputs. In the cycle, Q_net=Q_H−Q_C and W_net,out=Q_H−Q_C. For Q_H=900 J and Q_C=500 J, W_net,out=**400 J** (original independent instructional scenario, not a textbook example). A hypothetical single-reservoir cycle Q_H=600 J, W_net,out=600 J with no other effect satisfies first-law arithmetic but **violates Kelvin–Planck**.
- **Clausius statement:** cold-to-hot heat transfer cannot happen **as the only effect**. A refrigerator and heat pump transfer energy from cold to hot **with external work input** and hence do **not** violate it. Natural heat transfer from hotter to colder objects is consistent with the law.
- No student-facing theoretical claim that the Second Law forbids all cold-to-hot transfer; no invented full Carnot formula/COP/entropy coverage, and no implication that this is a graded or teacher-approved source exercise edition.
