'Tends to rise' needs clear interpretation. | **Keep source answer C for provisional practice; no silent mutation.** |

**Q72 deserves special care.** The question says a *water-filled mug* is moved from a swimming pool into air. Depending on whether it is open, its position, whether water is retained/drains, and whether the support force includes the water inside, the apparent-load calculation differs. D is the intended and best original answer among the four, **but the wording should not be certified as an unambiguous board-level physics question** without clarifying these conditions. **Compulsory teacher wording/alternate question review**; don't overwrite public source silently.

**Q79:** Enlarging a hull's possible displaced volume increases **available maximum buoyancy and carrying capability**. When a ship already floats at *unchanged weight* in the same liquid, `F_B=W`; modifying the hull does not force the *actual equilibrium buoyant force* to increase. This distinction is important for engineering understanding.

**Q70 and Q80:** Zero net force vs stationary motion, and net upward force vs instantaneous upward velocity, must remain conceptually distinct.
