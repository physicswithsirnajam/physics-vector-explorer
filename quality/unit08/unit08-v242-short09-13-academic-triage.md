## Only the qualifications needing teacher attention

| Original item | Compulsory or optional? | Reason and required precision | Keep the original? Add a new question? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8.9 — Can one process be both adiabatic and isothermal?** | **COMPULSORY explanation qualification; SLO mapping review** | **Adiabatic** refers to zero heat exchange, **isothermal** to constant temperature. An ideal gas freely expanding into a vacuum inside an insulated container has zero external heat and boundary work, giving the same initial and final *equilibrium* temperature, but its irreversible expansion is **not a reversible, quasistatic isothermal P–V path**. Avoid both an unconditional “never” and an unsupported reversible-free-expansion argument. | **Keep original** and add a careful, model-bounded teaching note. No substitute source question needed. |
| **8.10 — Entropy of a Carnot engine each cycle** | **COMPULSORY system/entropy distinction; SLO mapping review** | The cyclic **working fluid** returns to its initial state, so its **net entropy change per complete cycle is zero**. In a **reversible Carnot engine**, entropy changes of hot and cold reservoirs cancel, giving no total entropy generation. Distinguish this from irreversible engines, where entropy may be generated in the complete system+surroundings even though the working-fluid entropy is cyclic. | **Keep original**. Explain which system is being discussed; no replacement is required. |
| **8.11 — Friction and entropy** | **COMPULSORY entropy generation vs net subsystem change distinction** | Friction produces entropy; entropy of an **isolated whole** increases. But a system allowed to exchange heat or matter can export enough entropy that its **net** entropy does not necessarily rise. The source does not specify its exact boundaries, so avoid an unconditional assertion about every subsystem. | **Keep original** and clearly scope the result. No additional item needed. |
| **8.12 — Refrigerator versus air conditioner** | **OPTIONAL application note; no source amendment** | Both typically use a **reversed heat-engine/refrigeration cycle** to remove heat from a cold region using work. They differ mainly in target cooling application and often dehumidification; not in a universally distinct thermodynamic principle. | **Keep original**; a short comparisons table may help when an approved teacher answer edition is created. |
| **8.13 — Coffee-filled thermos shaken vigorously, parts (i)–(iv)** | **COMPULSORY completeness and idealisation clarification; SLO mapping review** | Investigate separately **(i) mechanical work delivered to coffee, (ii) internal-energy change after motion dissipates, (iii) possible small temperature rise, and (iv) negligible external heat for a well-insulated thermos**. Vigorous shaking does not mean external *heat* enters. Energy can be shared with the container, and an actual thermos is imperfectly insulated; do not assume all input work heats only coffee or promise a measurable rise. The four original prompts must never be silently collapsed. | **Keep original four-part question**. If a teaching scenario is added later, label it separately and get teacher approval. |

None of these five has a newly confirmed defective publisher stem that must be silently rewritten. The above **four compulsory explanation controls** prevent oversimplified marking; **Q8.12** is an optional teaching comparison.
