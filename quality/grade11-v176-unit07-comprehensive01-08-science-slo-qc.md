### Only questions needing an amendment or deliberate teacher decision

| Original item | Priority | Specific physics or assessment problem | May original remain? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7.1** | **Optional** | Polycrystalline solids **are crystalline within each grain**; this is a crystallographic grain distinction, not a chemical/polymer category. Amorphous materials have short-range order but lack long-range periodic order. | **Yes.** Keep the original and clarify single-crystal versus multiple grains in the model rubric; polymeric structure is a separate classification axis. |
| **7.2** | **Compulsory** | Calling **every** unit cell the "smallest repeat" is misleading: BCC and FCC **conventional** cells are larger than their primitive cells. SC/BCC/FCC refer to cubic lattice-point centering, whereas **seven crystal systems** classify cell geometry. A lattice point need not correspond to exactly one atom in a multi-atom basis. | **Yes.** Preserve the source prompt; teacher explanation must distinguish *primitive versus conventional unit cell*, **cubic cell types** versus **crystal systems**, and effective lattice points versus basis atoms. |
| **7.3** | **Optional** | Stress has Pa units; strain is dimensionless. Normal, shear and bulk examples use **corresponding** stresses and strains. Young's modulus as a *constant ratio* presumes the relevant proportional/linear elastic range. The source definition task is primarily **Understanding**, not automatic fulfilment of official B-24 **Application**. | **Yes.** Retain and improve assumptions/units in the explanatory key; add a separately reviewed applied case to assess B-24 directly. |
| **7.4** | **Compulsory** | A "ductile" engineering stress–strain graph does not have an identical yield plateau for every metal. Require correctly named axes (engineering stress versus engineering strain if that convention is used), distinguish proportional/elastic limits, yielding, ultimate **engineering** tensile stress, necking and fracture; avoid universal point-letter mappings. | **Yes.** Use a carefully labelled **schematic** model response, with material-dependent regions clearly stated. Do not claim every metal has distinct upper/lower yield points. |
| **7.5** | **Optional** | Recoverable elastic deformation may persist after the strict straight-line proportional regime but before the elastic limit; plastic deformation is **permanent set on unloading**, not automatically breakage. A loading sketch alone may not explain the residual strain. | **Yes.** Keep the prompt, include an unloading explanation and distinguish elastic limit from proportional limit. |
| **7.6** | **Compulsory** | `W=∫F dx` equals the geometrical area beneath an **F(vertical)–extension(horizontal)** graph. In NBF Fig.7.14 the weight/force and extension axes appear **reversed**; its printed "area under" wording is not a general nonlinear proof. Graph **gradient** represents stiffness (or reciprocal compliance if axes reversed), not work. | **Yes.** Derive `dW=F dx`, explicitly specify graph axes and integrate. Use `W=½Fx` only for a linear, zero-origin force-extension curve. |
| **7.7** | **Compulsory** | Recoverable stored elastic energy `U=½Fx=½kx²=YA(ΔL)²/(2L)` assumes **quasistatic zero-to-final loading in Hooke's-law range**. Outside it, a line triangle is not generally correct. The area under a **stress–strain** curve is **energy density** (J/m³), not necessarily total energy (J). | **Yes.** Preserve derivation prompt; enforce linear regime, zero initial extension, correct energy units, and distinguish force-extension work from stress–strain energy density. |
| **7.8** | **Compulsory** | The Young-modulus metallic-wire experiment needs matched original **gauge length**, diameter-based cross-sectional area, **force increment** and corresponding elongation. Some optional apparatus improvements (e.g., reference wire, repeats, graph gradient) must not be asserted as literal requirements of NBF Fig.7.13. | **Yes.** Keep the source; demand a labelled setup, method, `Y=FL/(AΔL)`, `A=πd²/4`, `F=mg` with a consistent initial-load baseline, elastic-limit check, SI units, and sensible measurement precautions. |
