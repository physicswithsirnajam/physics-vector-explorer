Upthrust and Archimedes’ Principle

Framework outcome(s): P-11-B-33 · P-11-B-34 · Topic-level guidance, not a certified individual MCQ mapping

01 / 07
01

Read

Buoyancy arises because pressure on a submerged body differs with depth. For a freely floating object in equilibrium, the upward buoyant force balances its weight.

Fᵦ = ρ(fluid) × g × V(displaced)

Remember: For a fully immersed rigid body in uniform-density fluid, displaced volume stays fixed. Floating equilibrium does not establish ship stability.

Think about this:

Can an immersed object experience upthrust even if it sinks?

Reveal explanation

Yes. Buoyancy acts on immersed objects; sinking means the downward forces exceed the buoyant force. Only at free-floating equilibrium do we set Fᵦ = W.

Viscous Drag and Terminal Velocity

Framework outcome(s): P-11-B-41 · P-11-B-42 · Topic-level guidance, not a certified individual MCQ mapping

02 / 07
01

Read

Real fluids oppose relative motion through viscous drag. A sphere may reach a steady terminal speed when its net force becomes zero.

Fᴅ = 6πηrv (Stokes model, creeping flow)

Remember: This model assumes a small sphere and very low Reynolds number; the prepared textbook's formal raindrop result is not a realistic high-speed aerodynamic prediction.

Fluid Flow

Framework outcome(s): P-11-B-35 · Topic-level guidance, not a certified individual MCQ mapping

03 / 07
01

Read

Compare steady, laminar, turbulent, incompressible and non-viscous flow. These are different properties, even when an introductory textbook discusses them together.

Q = ΔV / Δt (supplementary mean flow rate)

Remember: Steady means time-invariant at a fixed point; it does not mean uniform speed everywhere. Curved streamlines do not automatically imply turbulent or rotational flow.

Equation of Continuity

Framework outcome(s): P-11-B-36 · P-11-B-37 · P-11-B-38 · Topic-level guidance, not a certified individual MCQ mapping

04 / 07
01

Read

Mass conservation connects flow rates through different cross-sections of a pipe. For one steady, non-leaking stream the mass passing each section per second is the same.

ρ₁A₁v₁ = ρ₂A₂v₂; if ρ is constant: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

Remember: Sum the flows of all outlets at a junction. The flow rate in a real hose can change after squeezing; continuity alone does not calculate pressure.

A pipe divides into three branches. Is inlet flow equal to each branch separately?

Reveal explanation

No. Conservation requires the sum of all outgoing mass flow rates to equal the incoming rate (assuming no accumulation or leakage).

Bernoulli’s Equation

Framework outcome(s): P-11-B-40 · P-11-B-39 (formative background) · Topic-level guidance, not a certified individual MCQ mapping

05 / 07
01

Read

For suitable ideal steady flow along one streamline, pressure work, kinetic energy and gravitational energy contribute to a conserved energy-per-volume sum.

p + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

Remember: Identify the reference height, static versus gauge/absolute pressure, and losses. P-11-B-39 is formative; P-11-B-40 is summative at outcome level.

Superfluidity

Framework outcome(s): P-11-B-43 · Topic-level guidance, not a certified individual MCQ mapping

07 / 07
01

Read

At certain low-temperature conditions helium can show superfluid phenomena including thin-film wall creep and persistent quantized circulation.

Concept focus: zero ordinary viscous resistance (idealization)

Remember: Zero viscosity does not mean zero gravity or no other interactions. Supplementary temperature calculations are not required B43 derivations. Original NBF Figure 6.17 is not reproduced here.
