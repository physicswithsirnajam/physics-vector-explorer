Teaching insight

An object with constant positive acceleration has a straight rising velocity–time graph and a curved position–time graph.

Important: The signed area under a velocity–time graph equals displacement. Distance is the area ignoring negative signs.

Part A · Observe and explain

1. Set v₀ = 4 m/s and a = 0 m/s². Describe the velocity–time and position–time graphs.

2. Set v₀ = 2 m/s and a = +2 m/s². What does the slope of the velocity–time graph represent?

3. Set v₀ = 6 m/s and a = −3 m/s². At what time does the car change direction? How can you tell from the graphs?

Part B · Calculate and verify

4. An object starts at x₀ = 0 m, with v₀ = 3 m/s and a = 2 m/s². Calculate its velocity and displacement after 4 s.

5. For v₀ = 6 m/s and a = −3 m/s², calculate displacement and distance in the first 5 s. Explain the difference.

6. Why does the signed area of a velocity–time graph equal displacement rather than total distance?
