| **8.7 — relative steepness of adiabatic and isothermal P–V curves** | **COMPULSORY shared-state and ideal-gas model qualification**, plus candidate SLO review | On the **same P–V axes at the same state** for a quasistatic perfect ideal gas, isothermal tangent slope is **−P/V**, while a reversible adiabatic tangent is **−γP/V**, with **γ > 1**. This supports a larger absolute slope for the adiabat; it is not a universal comparison of arbitrary states or substances. The closest printed SLO **P-11-C-10** covers the constant-temperature P–V graph, not explicitly the adiabatic slope proof. | **Keep original.** Add a separate original curve-comparison visual only if teacher requests it and independently validates the plot. |
| **8.8 — why isothermal is typically slow and adiabatic typically fast** | **COMPULSORY distinction between heuristic and thermodynamic definition**, plus direct summative SLO review | Slow movement can allow a system to exchange heat with an environment and maintain approximately fixed temperature; a fast change often limits heat exchange. **But speed is not the defining criterion**: isothermal means constant temperature, adiabatic means no heat transfer. A slow, thermally insulated process remains adiabatic, and an efficiently controlled faster process may remain isothermal. This qualification was already flagged in V235. | **Keep original with explicit “typically.”** Optionally add an independently authored timescale/boundary scenario after approval; do not force a direct SLO without evidence. |
