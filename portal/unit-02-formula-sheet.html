P-11-B-01 — Rectangular components

Vector notation

A⃗ = Aₓî + Aᵧĵ

Represent a two-dimensional vector in Cartesian components.

Resolve when θ is measured from +x

Aₓ = A cos θ; Aᵧ = A sin θ

Choose the sign using the vector quadrant.

Magnitude

A = √(Aₓ² + Aᵧ²)

Magnitude is always non-negative.

Direction

tan θ = Aᵧ/Aₓ

Use x and y signs to identify the correct quadrant; simple inverse tangent gives a reference angle.

Position vector

r⃗ = aî + bĵ; |r⃗| = √(a² + b²)

For position P(a,b), the components are (a,b).

P-11-B-02 — Scalar / dot product

Definition

A⃗ · B⃗ = AB cos θ

Dot product is a scalar.

Component method

A⃗ · B⃗ = AₓBₓ + AᵧBᵧ + A_zB_z

Multiply matching components and add.

Angle from dot product

cos θ = (A⃗ · B⃗)/(AB)

For two non-zero vectors; the sign of cos θ indicates acute or obtuse.

Scalar projection

comp_B A = (A⃗ · B⃗)/|B⃗|

Project A onto the direction of B, provided B is non-zero.

Special cases

Parallel: AB; perpendicular: 0; antiparallel: −AB

Commutative: A⃗ · B⃗ = B⃗ · A⃗; self-product A².

P-11-B-02 — Vector / cross product

Definition

A⃗ × B⃗ = AB sin θ n̂

The direction is normal to the A–B plane; right-hand rule applies.

Anti-commutative

A⃗ × B⃗ = −(B⃗ × A⃗)

Changing the order reverses direction.

Unit-vector cycle

î × ĵ = k̂; ĵ × k̂ = î; k̂ × î = ĵ

Reverse order changes signs; same-vector cross products vanish.

3-D components

(AᵧB_z−A_zBᵧ)î + (A_zBₓ−AₓB_z)ĵ + (AₓBᵧ−AᵧBₓ)k̂

Equivalent to the 3×3 determinant rule.

2-D shortcut

A⃗ × B⃗ = (AₓBᵧ−AᵧBₓ)k̂

Use for vectors in the xy-plane.

Parallelogram area

Area = |A⃗ × B⃗| = AB sin θ

The area is non-negative and has area units.

Integrated relations and applications

Products to angle

tan θ = |A⃗ × B⃗| / (A⃗ · B⃗)

Use the sign of the dot product and handle a zero dot product separately; the ratio alone can obscure the quadrant.

Recover AB

AB = √[(A⃗ · B⃗)² + |A⃗ × B⃗|²]

AB is the product of vector magnitudes.

Work and power

W = F⃗ · d⃗; P = F⃗ · v⃗

Scalar products.

Torque and angular momentum

τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗; L⃗ = r⃗ × p⃗

Vector products.

On an axis, one component is zero; e.g., 0° → (A,0), 90° → (0,A), 180° → (−A,0), 270° → (0,−A).

High-value examiner tips

Quadrants: An inverse-tangent calculator value may give only a reference angle. Determine the final direction from the signs of the components.

Dot versus cross: Dot product uses cosine and gives a scalar; cross product uses sine and gives a vector.

Product ratios: A positive or negative dot product matters when finding the angle from dot/cross magnitudes. Avoid quoting only the acute reference angle.

Vector order: A⃗ × B⃗ is not the same as B⃗ × A⃗; its direction reverses.

Units: Include N for force, N·m for work/torque as relevant, W for power, and square units for geometric area.
