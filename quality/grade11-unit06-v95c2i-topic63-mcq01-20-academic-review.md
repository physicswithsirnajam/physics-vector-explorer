### Important distinctions for teaching

**Steady vs laminar (Q5, Q7).** A **steady** velocity field means velocity at a fixed location does not change with time. **Laminar** describes orderly layered flow without the broad fluctuating eddies characteristic of turbulence. They are **not universally equivalent**: laminar flow can be unsteady. The owner-prepared textbook introduction uses the phrase “steady or laminar” informally; retain the author's intended answers while explicitly distinguishing these concepts in teacher notes.

**Tube-center speed (Q8).** A central layer tends to move faster than near-wall layers under a developed pressure-driven no-slip laminar profile in a tube; it is not a theorem about every laminar tube flow.

**Critical speed (Q9, Q12).** Lower speed often promotes laminar flow in an otherwise comparable arrangement. There is no single universal critical speed; geometry, fluid properties, Reynolds conditions and disturbances matter.

**Incompressible vs inviscid (Q14–19).** Incompressibility is the simplified constant-density assumption; inviscid or non-viscous means viscosity is neglected. Neither implies zero mass, zero pressure or fluid at rest.

**Flow-rate terminology (Q2, Q20).** `Q=V/t` and `[Q]=m³/s` are mathematically and dimensionally correct and are present as supporting introductory content. The exact official B-35 target lists *steady, incompressible, non-viscous ideal flow* terms, so such items should not automatically be certified as B-35 summative board-paper questions. They remain legitimate self-practice questions.
