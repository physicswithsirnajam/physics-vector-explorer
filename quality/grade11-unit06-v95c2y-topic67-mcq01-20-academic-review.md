### Scientific interpretation and exam-scope guidance
- **Q1–4 core superfluid concept:** Zero viscosity is a flow-resistance property, **not** zero mass, zero density, zero speed or zero gravity. Ordinary viscous damping is absent in the ideal superfluid limit, and vortices can persist. Q1/Q2 are Knowledge-like recognition questions; Q3/Q4 demonstrate more of the intended B43 Understanding. Keep all four for formative/revision practice and certify at individual question level.
- **Q5 and original Figure6.16:** The printed p106 figure visually depicts a superfluid creeping over the container wall. Original B is source-faithful. However Q5 says *"Which phenomenon is illustrated by NBF Figure6.16?"* and the public bank record has **no attached `referenceFigureId`**. Having checked the printed original does **not** prove the portal/student sees that figure. A teacher can either attach a lawful faithful accessible visual or write a self-contained alternative after explicit approval.
- **Q8/Q9 numerical helium transitions:** The original NBF p106 information box quotes **2.17 K** for helium-4 and **0.0025 K (2.5 mK)** for helium-3. They are approximate state/pressure-dependent thresholds, **not universal pressure-independent constants**. The source answer letters A and B are correct in that printed-book context. Memorizing threshold numbers is not expressly prescribed by B43.
- **Q10–12 and original Figure6.17:** Actual graph axes **Temperature(K)** horizontal, **Pressure(MPa)** vertical, and labels solid/normal liquid/superfluid/gas are printed and confirmed. The page's tiny graph requires legibility if reproduced; neither original textbook image nor a student portal figure link is supplied in this PR. Label/axis identification is mostly Knowledge recall; direct graph comparison could assess deeper Understanding if separately designed and approved.
- **Q13–16 subcritical rotating helium/quantized vortices:** The original p106 text says an ideal superfluid can remain stationary while a container turns slowly, then nucleate a quantized vortex at critical angular velocity; vortex circulation comes in discrete allowed strengths, unlike classical water rotation. These are scientific concept extensions consistent with the textbook, though the official B43 examples emphasize persistent vortices rather than deriving quantum circulation formulae. Avoid universal claims about every finite-temperature, frictional or container-prepared setting.
- **Q17–20 advanced named uses:** The original NBF p106 lists high-precision gyroscopes, detecting tiny gravitational effects, trapping/slowing light and possible superfluid neutron-fluid interiors. All four answers are **faithful to the printed source**, but these are specialized enrichment factual recognition rather than the express zero-viscosity/creep/persistent-vortex Understanding target. In particular **Q20 says *may***, not a universal established neutron-star measurement, and Q19 is a textbook attribution, not a claim that any liquid can trap light.
- **Scope safety:** Q5/Q10/Q11/Q12 use **printed figure numbers** but `referenceFigureId` is absent in their current GitHub source objects; figure provenance and legal student-facing illustration availability require review independent of the scientifically correct source key.
