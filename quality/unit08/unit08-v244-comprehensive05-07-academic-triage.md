## Independent science checks that must govern eventual solutions

- The First Law for a complete *cyclic* working substance has \(\Delta U_{\rm cycle}=0\). For a noncyclic stage, retaining a nonzero \(\Delta U\) is essential; the equality \(Q_H-Q_C=W_{\rm net}\) cannot be imposed without cycle/energy-balance conditions.
- Two finite positive absolute reservoir temperatures satisfy \(T_H>T_C>0\). A heat engine needs a heat sink in the two-reservoir cyclic model; the source's broad hot/cold diagram should not be mistaken for a universal physical inventory of every engine.
- Kelvin–Planck and Clausius statements must be explained as **cyclic/sole-effect restrictions**, distinguishing non-spontaneous cold-to-hot transfer with work from forbidden uncompensated transfer.
- The source explicitly shows isothermal expansion → adiabatic expansion → isothermal compression → adiabatic compression. **Reversible** is essential for the Carnot heat-temperature ratio and maximum efficiency. More general engines still satisfy energy conservation but do not automatically satisfy \(Q_C/Q_H=T_C/T_H\).
- Original Fig. 8.16 is the \(P\)–\(V\) loop and Fig. 8.17 is the four-stroke ideal cycle schematic; dropping either from a visual explanation without an independently validated equivalent can mislead about states and paths. The original comprehensive prompt does **not** print subquestion letters requiring artificial '(a)–(d)' numbering; the **four process steps are chapter explanations**, not separately numbered exercise subparts.
