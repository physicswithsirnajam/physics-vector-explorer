Experiment 1 · Water waves passing through a slit

These are plane waves approaching two barriers with an adjustable opening. Compare the slit width a with the wavelength λ. Watch the emerging wavefronts bend around the slit edges.

Experiment controls

Student prediction

Keep wavelength fixed. What happens to spreading if you gradually decrease the gap width?

A small gap relative to wavelength produces conspicuous spreading. A large gap produces a mostly forward-directed wavefront with diffraction concentrated near the edges.

Experiment 2 · Predict which opening bends waves more

Compare two openings exposed to the same wavelength. Use the values, sketch the expected fronts, and test which case shows stronger spreading. Remember: the relative size matters.

Case A · Narrow opening

Slit width a / wavelength λ45 / 50 = 0.90

Slit approximately equal to wavelength → emerging fronts spread considerably.

Case B · Wide opening

Slit width a / wavelength λ180 / 50 = 3.60

Slit much wider than wavelength → much of the transmitted wave remains nearly plane.

FBISE question: Does a greater frequency automatically mean stronger diffraction? No. For a fixed wave speed, raising frequency decreases wavelength, making the same slit relatively wider and reducing the visible spreading.
