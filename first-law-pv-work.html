Sign convention

Q positive: heat is added to gas. Q negative: heat leaves gas.

W positive: gas expands and does work on the surroundings. W negative: surroundings compress the gas and do work on it.

ΔU positive: internal energy rises. ΔU negative: internal energy falls.

Textbook Example 8.3 (p. 139–140): If ΔU = −200 J and the gas does +50 J of work, then Q = −150 J. The system releases 150 J of heat. This exactly matches Q = ΔU + W.

We explicitly use the textbook convention work done BY the gas. If a problem gives work done ON the gas instead, reverse its sign before entering W.

Compare four thermodynamic processes

All comparisons use an ideal monatomic gas. Isothermal, isobaric and adiabatic comparison paths use the same initial state and chosen final volume. Isochoric uses the same initial state but changes temperature at constant volume.

Key difference: A constant-temperature ideal-gas process has ΔU = 0, but Q and W need not be zero. An adiabatic process has Q = 0, but temperature and internal energy may change. A constant-volume process has W = 0 even when heat is transferred.

Short worked derivation · P–V work

For a gas pushing a piston of cross-sectional area A against external pressure P, the force is F = PA. A small piston displacement dx changes the gas volume by dV = A dx.

dW = F dx = PA dx = P dV
W = ∫ P dV

When pressure remains constant, this reduces to W = P(V₂ − V₁). On a pressure–volume graph, the signed area under the path is the work done by the gas. Expansion is positive and compression is negative.

The integral and the ideal monatomic-gas comparison equations are explanatory extensions; the core NBF treatment emphasizes W = PΔV, the P–V area and the four named processes.
