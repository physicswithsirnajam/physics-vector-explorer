### Physics reasoning and conditions

1. **Horizontal pressure–speed statements (Q21, Q22, Q25, Q26):** Under ideal same-streamline, steady incompressible no-loss/no-shaft-work conditions at equal heights, `P+½ρv²=constant`. For an unbranched tube with constant Q, continuity further yields higher mean speed in a narrower section and lower static pressure. **Q25 does not explicitly state negligible viscosity or no external work**; keep option B as the source-best classroom answer but amend/clarify the modelling conditions for certified exam use. **Q26** assumes ideal flow but real diffusers can have pressure losses. The isolated Bernoulli effect is B39 formative; equations explicitly applied under B40 can be evaluated separately.
2. **Vertical elevation changes (Q23, Q24):** At equal fluid speed, an ideal rising flow loses pressure as `ρgh` rises, while descending fluid at unchanged speed gains pressure; source D and A, respectively, are correct.
3. **Full equation limits (Q27–32):** The height contribution cancels between equal-elevation points; it does not imply gravity vanishes. The correct relation includes pressure, kinetic and elevation terms. Viscous dissipation (Q31) and pump shaft work (Q32) require extended energy balances, and the unmodified Bernoulli constant is not universal across unrelated or nonideal flows.
4. **Conserved sum and algebra (Q33–38):** The energy-per-unit-volume sum is constant along the ideal streamline, not each term separately. At fixed density `½ρv²∝v²` and at fixed v it is proportional to density; **Q35's doubling of ρ is a comparison of conditions/fluids rather than a density change along one incompressible streamline**. The horizontal Bernoulli difference obeys `P₁−P₂=½ρ(v₂²−v₁²)`, original Q37 option A (correct sign). Same pressure but different heights can be balanced by different speeds where physically feasible.
5. **Change in energies (Q39, Q40):** `ΔKE=½m(v₂²−v₁²)` and `ΔPE=mg(h₂−h₁)` are correct foundation formulas. Alone, these do not establish an Application-level explanation of the pressure/velocity/height Bernoulli equation under official B40.
