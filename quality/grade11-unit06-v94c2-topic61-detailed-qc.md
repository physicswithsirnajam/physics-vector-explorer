### Part B: 52-question first-pass scientific review

- **Q1–Q13 (foundations):** Fluid types, upthrust, `F_B=ρ_fgV_displaced`, apparent weight, submerged rise/sink/suspension and freely floating equilibrium. The given formulas and signs agree with the hydrostatic model. `F_B=W` is an equilibrium statement—not a general equation for every immersed or accelerating object. **Q13** apparent weight assumes a stationary supported body with no additional vertical contact forces and negligible air buoyancy.
- **Q14–Q26 (reasoning):** Figure6.2 force directions and `(p₂−p₁)A`; equal displaced volumes; depth independence in uniform-density fluid; fluid density vs object density. These are mutually consistent when top/bottom areas are equal and hydrostatic conditions apply. `p=ρgh` is **gauge** pressure relative to open free surface, so common atmospheric terms cancel. **Q21** depth independence assumes a rigid fully submerged body, constant fluid density and g.
- **Q27–Q39 (13 quantitative items):** Numerics independently recalculated (see below), with **Q38** clearly distinguished as an original **Assignment6.1-inspired** application in Part B rather than a second newly printed assignment. The formula uses **fluid** density for buoyancy, iron/object density for true weight; `1 g/cm³=1000 kg/m³` and `3 L=0.003 m³` conversions are correct.
- **Q40–Q52 (ships, submarines and stability):** Buoyancy and large displacement, cargo, safe capacity, ballast, material density vs whole-vessel mean density are physically sensible at the intended school level. The notes already warn that floating (`F_B=W`) does not automatically mean stable or seaworthy. Low mass placement assists stability, but a full righting-moment analysis requires hull geometry. Submarine ballast explanations assume approximately fixed exterior submerged volume. Do not certify a real hull's safety using buoyancy alone.
