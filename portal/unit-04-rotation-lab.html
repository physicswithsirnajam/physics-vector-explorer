Rotational Kinematics Laboratory

Learn radians visually, change the rotation of a wheel, inspect angular velocity and displacement graphs, and reproduce the NBF textbook's bicycle tyre example. All calculations occur locally on this page.

Model assumptions: This simulator describes rotation about a fixed axis and constant angular acceleration. Anti-clockwise is positive. Signed angular displacement is different from total angle travelled when a wheel reverses. Tangential acceleration is only one component of acceleration. No centripetal-force simulation, student grading, account login or saved results are included in this first Unit 4 release.

1. Radian and arc-length explorer

A radian is the angle that subtends an arc equal in length to the circle's radius. Set any signed rotation from −360° to +360° and adjust the physical radius.

A negative signed arc displacement represents clockwise motion; the arc distance is its positive magnitude. For one complete revolution, position returns to the initial ray even though angular displacement is ±2π rad.

This model uses a fixed axis. The gold spoke tracks angular position modulo one turn; the numerical Δθ and revolution count preserve complete turns. The wheel may look stationary after an exact whole-number revolution even though it has rotated. Angular acceleration is constant, not necessarily zero.

Textbook Example 4.1: radius 0.331 m, initial linear speed 6.80 m/s, travel 74.8 m. From unrounded values the wheel stops after about 22.0 s. The printed book reports ≈20.54 rad/s, 35.9 revolutions, −0.94 rad/s² and 21.9 s, with rounded intermediate values; small last-digit differences are expected.
