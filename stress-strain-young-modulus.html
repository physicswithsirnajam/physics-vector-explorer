Virtual Young's modulus experiment
  1. Measure original length L and wire diameter d using appropriate instruments.
  2. Calculate A = πd²/4. Add known loads; measure ΔL after each load.
  3. Plot F against ΔL; its elastic slope is k = YA/L.
  4. Calculate Y = kL/A and check recovery on unloading.

Elastic and plastic deformation — What happens beyond the elastic region?

Follow an idealized tensile test. Compare a ductile material with a brittle material.

The force and extension initially rise together. The proportional elastic region is approximately linear.

Observe these landmarks

Elastic / proportional region

Stress is proportional to strain. On removing the load, the material returns to its original dimensions.

Yielding and plastic deformation

Beyond the elastic limit, unloading may leave permanent strain. Young's modulus cannot be used to extrapolate the entire plastic curve.

Ultimate strength and fracture

A ductile specimen may undergo substantial plastic deformation before fracture. Brittle specimens tend to fracture with much less plastic deformation.
