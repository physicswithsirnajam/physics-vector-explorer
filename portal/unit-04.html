Grade XI · FBISE · Unit 04

Rotational and Circular Motion

Explore rotating wheels, inward centripetal acceleration, astronauts freely falling in orbit and the artificial-gravity effect created by a rotating space station. Work through three source-based study stages and interactive laboratories.

Publication stage: Unit 4 Parts 1–3 cover rotational kinematics (pp.54–59), centripetal motion and force (pp.59–64), and weightlessness/artificial gravity (pp.71–74). These are public source-based teaching resources. Moment of inertia, torque and angular momentum remain for later releases. No Unit 4 MCQ bank, descriptive assessment or saved student score has been published.

FBISE source and assessment boundaries

Primary source: NBF Grade XI Physics, Unit 4 — Rotational and Circular Motion, printed p.53 chapter opening; pp.54–59 rotational kinematics; pp.59–64 centripetal acceleration, force, and applications; and §§4.6–4.7, pp.71–73, satellite weightlessness and artificial gravity (plus Assignment 4.4 on p.74). Official assessment classifications follow the supplied FBISE HSSC-I Physics Assessment Framework.

  • P-11-B-07 — Summative (covered): Express angles in radians.
  • P-11-B-08 — Summative (covered): Calculate angular displacement, angular velocity, angular acceleration and related linear/angular quantities.
  • P-11-B-09 — Summative (covered): Apply equations of rotational motion to problems.
  • P-11-B-10 — Summative (Part 2 covered): Qualitative analysis of curved motion due to a perpendicular, inward force.
  • P-11-B-14 — Summative (Part 2 covered): Calculate centripetal force using F = mv²/r = mrω².
  • P-11-B-15 — Summative (Part 2 covered): Analyse inward force supplied by tension, friction, gravitation and normal reaction, including a banked road.
  • P-11-B-16 — Summative (Part 3 covered): Explain weightlessness and near-zero apparent support force for occupants of orbiting spacecraft.
  • P-11-B-17 — Summative (Part 3 covered): Explain and calculate artificial-gravity effects in an ideal rotating station.
  • P-11-B-18 onward — Summative (later stages): Moment of inertia, torque, angular momentum and conservation of angular momentum.

Parts 1–3 do not claim complete chapter coverage, a summative question bank or verified full-syllabus exam readiness. The simulations run locally without saving student progress or accessing enrolled accounts.
