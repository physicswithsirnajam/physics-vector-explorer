## Science and assessment boundaries

**Q7.1:** under the ideal simple-shear assumption, `γ=Δx/ℓ`, `τ=F/A`, and `G=τ/γ`. The printed elastic constant is **E (Young modulus)**; `G` requires an additional relation and Poisson's ratio. An actual protruding metal nail under a sideways load would ordinarily experience bending as well as shear; using pure uniform shear is a pedagogical approximation. The original conditional hint is not a uniquely derived quantity without an added assumption.

**Q7.2:** `ΔL=FL/(YA)` and the same `F,L,ΔL` imply the required **cross-sectional area** scales inversely with Young modulus and the diameter with its square root. This result has been independently checked and is the single clean agreement.

**Q7.3:** common-area wire segments under serial hanging masses cannot use the same tension in both segments; upper support takes the total hanging weight. The lower segment's smaller printed modulus dominates elongation. For strain energy, use `U_s+U_b`, and distinguish stored energy from work at differing load points. **Energy has units joules; extensions have metres.**

**Q7.4:** the area in mm² must become m², and the bar length contributes to stored energy through volume. Two independent formula routes agree, reinforcing the discrepancy rather than a rounding issue. Assume the prescribed stress remains in the proportional elastic regime.

**Q7.5:** the source explicitly says a diameter **in centimetres**, not millimetres; the squared-area effect can create a two-order-of-magnitude stress error. Preserve printed diameter in the teacher calculation and annotate the likely mistaken publisher hint.
