### Scientific teaching distinctions

- **Q21–24:** nonintersecting streamlines require well-defined local direction; a narrow smooth dye streak is consistent with ordered flow; sudden geometry change *may* promote turbulence. Q24's panel order is supported by the prepared teacher PDF, **not directly rechecked against the printed NBF original Figure6.6**.
- **Q25–27:** in a pressure-driven developed no-slip laminar tube profile, speed is generally greater toward the center; it is not required for all laminar arrangements. The critical transition to turbulence is flow/Reynolds/geometry dependent, and an isolated coherent vortex alone is not sufficient proof of turbulence.
- **Q28–30, Q34–35, Q39–40:** an ideal fluid may be modeled incompressible (constant density) and inviscid (neglected viscous resistance); real fluids have viscosity and water has nonzero compressibility that is usually small in ordinary classroom problems. These concepts directly support B-35.
- **Q31–33:** a streamlined profile often reduces flow disturbance and drag; wind-tunnel engineering, and dolphin shape, are useful real-world learning applications but **may exceed B-35's narrow list of ideal-fluid definitions**. Alternative candidate B-42 alignment is only a suggestion for teacher review, **not an official eligibility approval**. Q33's printed dolphin information-box attribution remains unchecked.
- **Q36:** honey is generally more viscous than water under comparable pouring conditions, but its real-fluid viscosity is more closely associated with **B-41** than B-35. Preserve the original B answer for practice pending item-level framework mapping.
- **Q37–38:** laminar or steady need not mean uniform speed everywhere; blunt obstacles can produce wake disturbances and eddies depending on the regime.
