From observation to equation

The inverse-square relationship

F = k |q₁q₂| / r² k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² 1 nC = 10⁻⁹ C
At fixed charges: 2r → F/4, 3r → F/9, and r/2 → 4F. Charge signs determine attraction or repulsion, not the magnitude in this equation.

Graph challenge

Try q₁ = +3 nC and q₂ = −5 nC. Move from 0.50 m to 1.00 m. Predict the ratio before moving the slider.

Why does doubling just one charge double the force, while doubling the distance reduces force by a factor of four?

What this model does not include

The source charges are treated as points and do not move in response to one another. At extremely small separations the ideal formula is not an appropriate model for extended objects.

Conceptual MCQs

What the arrows mean

F⃗(T ← i) = k qT qi (r⃗T − r⃗i) / |r⃗T − r⃗i|³ F⃗net = Σ F⃗i Fx,net = Σ Fx,i ; Fy,net = Σ Fy,i |F⃗net| = √(Fx² + Fy²) θ = atan2(Fy, Fx)

The signed product qTqi automatically determines attraction or repulsion. The resultant arrow is the vector sum, not the sum of the individual magnitudes.

Suggested 15-minute lesson

Engage (2 min): Ask whether equal forces always cancel.

Explore (5 min): Try balanced forces, then move one source slightly.

Explain (4 min): Show the x/y table and calculate the resultant.

Evaluate (4 min): Have learners solve two MCQs and one numerical independently.

Source and assumptions

NBF XI Unit 10: Coulomb's law, electric fields and field strength, pages 184–196 (relevant section). FBISE: P-11-E-07 and P-11-E-08 are summative. Point-charge force-vector composition and acceleration are teaching extensions; they reinforce, rather than replace, core content.

No friction, polarization of nearby bodies, or motion of source charges is modeled. Electric interactions are instantaneous static snapshots. Units are SI internally; the interface uses nanocoulombs and nanonewtons for convenient classroom values.
