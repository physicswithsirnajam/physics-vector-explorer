- **Compulsory explanation correction:** the original one-line explanation asserts nylon is *listed* as synthetic. NBF p.114 actually names **polythene, polystyrene and PVC**, not nylon; nylon is a correct synthetic-polymer example generally, but the exact book-attribution is not accurate.
- **Strict-source option improvement:** replace distractor “Nylon” with “Polythene,” a synthetic polymer explicitly named on p.114; keep original option ordering and intended answer intact. Since that changes an owner's option, obtain teacher approval and preserve source-change history.
- **Approval:** PENDING.

**Q15 · NBF-only source attribution**

- **Source intent/key:** sulphur is the intended choice; the original teacher explanation correctly says it is one of the NBF's *crystalline nonmetal* examples.
- **Problem:** Original question adds the phrase “molecular crystalline solid,” which the cited NBF page does not explicitly use for sulphur. Describing common sulphur crystals in chemistry as molecular is a defensible *external scientific fact*, but the label should not be attributed to this passage without qualification.
- **Compulsory for strict textbook-core publication:** Use V147's proposed question: “Which of the following nonmetal crystalline materials is listed in the NBF textbook?” Retain all four options in their original order; its intended sulphur choice remains unambiguous.
- **Optional alternative:** Keep the original molecular-crystal version only in a distinctly labelled *supplementary extension*, verified against a separate chemistry source, rather than as an extra near-duplicate core MCQ.
- **Approval:** Still **PENDING**; source Word untouched.

**Q62 · scientific precision for amorphous glass**

- **Source intent/key:** amorphous solid; its explanation identifies glass as amorphous.
- **Problem:** Original “random long-range arrangement” could incorrectly suggest a distinct long-range arrangement. The NBF describes an amorphous solid by the **absence of regular long-range repeating order**, although it may have short-range order.
- **Compulsory for public scored practice:** Use V147's proposed stem: “A glass pane has no regular long-range repeating arrangement of constituent particles. The best description is:”. The same four original options remain correctly directed at amorphous solid.
- **Keep-and-add?** **No.** Don't duplicate a misleading original wording in the standard student bank.
- **Approval:** Still **PENDING**; source Word untouched.

- **Compulsory correction:** use the NBF-listed material **polythene** in the question's scenario and its relevant choices; change the intended statement to “Polythene is listed as a synthetic polymer.” Verify the other distractors remain false.
