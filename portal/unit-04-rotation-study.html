Rotational Kinematics — Study Guide

Learn how angle, angular velocity and angular acceleration describe a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis. Connect the angular quantities to linear motion at the rim using your NBF textbook's first worked example.

P-11-B-07 · SUMMATIVE

1. Angles in radians

The radian is defined through an angle's arc: one radian subtends an arc whose length equals the radius. For a circle of radius r and arc length s:

θ (rad) = s / r    ⇒    s = rθ

The angle θ must be in radians when using s = rθ. One complete revolution corresponds to 2π radians, or 360°.

θ(rad) = θ(°) × π/180    |    1 rad ≈ 57.3°

Practice: a wheel has radius 0.40 m and turns 90°. What rim arc length corresponds to this turn?

θ = 90°×π/180 = π/2 rad. The magnitude of arc displacement is s = rθ = 0.40×π/2 ≈ 0.628 m.

P-11-B-08 · SUMMATIVE

2. Angular position, displacement, velocity and acceleration

Use a reference ray through the centre of a rotating body. Angular position θ describes the direction of a radius; angular displacement Δθ is the change in angular position. For a fixed rotation axis, take anticlockwise as positive and clockwise as negative.

Δθ = θ₂ − θ₁    (rad) Average angular velocity: ω̄ = Δθ / Δt    (rad/s) Instantaneous angular velocity: ω = dθ/dt Average angular acceleration: ᾱ = Δω / Δt    (rad/s²) Instantaneous angular acceleration: α = dω/dt

Angular velocity and acceleration have an axis direction specified by the right-hand rule: curling the fingers with the rotation gives the direction of the thumb. Their scalar signed components can be positive or negative under the chosen direction convention.

Relations between linear and angular quantities

Arc displacement s = rθ    |    Signed tangential speed vₜ = rω Tangential acceleration aₜ = rα

Here r is the fixed radius. The tangential component aₜ is not automatically the entire acceleration: in curved motion a radial component may also be present (covered in the centripetal stage).

Practice: a disc of radius 0.25 m rotates at +8 rad/s. Find the tangential speed at the rim.

vₜ = rω = (0.25)(+8) = +2.0 m/s along the positive tangential direction at the chosen point. Its speed magnitude is 2.0 m/s.

P-11-B-09 · SUMMATIVE

3. Equations of rotational motion at constant angular acceleration

When angular acceleration α is constant, rotational motion is governed by equations analogous to the familiar straight-line kinematic equations. With angular displacement measured relative to the start of the selected interval:

ω = ω₀ + αt Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt² ω² = ω₀² + 2αΔθ Δθ = ½(ω₀ + ω)t

These relations use signed quantities. Angular displacement and total angular distance are not necessarily equal if the body stops and reverses direction.

Practice: a wheel begins at +6 rad/s, slows at −2 rad/s², and is observed 4 s later. Find its final angular velocity, signed angular displacement, and total angle travelled.

ω = 6−2(4) = −2 rad/s. Δθ = 6(4)+½(−2)(4²) = +8 rad. It first stops at t=3 s after turning +9 rad, then reverses and turns −1 rad; the total angular distance is 10 rad, although displacement is +8 rad.

NBF WORKED EXAMPLE 4.1 · PRINTED PP.58–59

4. Bicycle tyre slowing to rest

A bicycle tyre of radius 33.1 cm (0.331 m) rolls at initial linear velocity 6.80 m/s and travels 74.8 m before coming to rest. Assume no slipping and uniform angular deceleration.

  1. Initial angular velocity: ω₀ = v/r = 6.80/0.331 ≈ 20.54 rad/s.
  2. Angle and revolutions: Δθ = s/r = 74.8/0.331 ≈ 226 rad, so N = Δθ/(2π) ≈ 35.97 rev using full input precision.
  3. Angular acceleration: 0 = ω₀² + 2αΔθ, so α = −ω₀²/(2Δθ) ≈ −0.934 rad/s².
  4. Time to stop: 0 = ω₀ + αt, so t = −ω₀/α ≈ 22.0 s.
Textbook rounding: The printed solution reports approximately 35.9 revolutions, −0.94 rad/s² and 21.9 seconds. Small differences from the unrounded calculations above arise because the textbook rounds intermediate values. Both representations describe the same stopping process.
