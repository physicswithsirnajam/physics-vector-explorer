Work from a force–displacement graph

The area under a graph of the force component along the displacement, F∥, gives signed work. Above the axis: positive; below the axis: negative. The model force changes linearly from F0 to F1.

Derive kinetic energy from motion

For a constant net force along straight-line motion, Fnet = ma and v² − u² = 2ad. Net work Fnetd = mad = ½m(v² − u²).

K = ½mv²   |   Wnet = ΔK
Initial kinetic energy K₀
Final kinetic energy K₁
Net work ΔK
Speed ratio v/u
Why v²? Because the work equation contains the change in squared speed, not just the change in speed. If u = 0, the net work needed to reach speed v is K = ½mv². A speed ratio is undefined when u = 0, but each kinetic energy remains well-defined.

Conservative gravity vs path-dependent resistance

Compare two routes between the same endpoints, from h = 10 m to h = 4 m. For gravity, work depends on vertical drop only. For a constant opposing resistive force, work depends on the total distance travelled.

Work–energy theorem in a resistive medium

For forward applied force Fa and opposing constant resistance Fr over displacement d, ΔK = (Fa − Fr)d. The work done against resistance is a positive energy requirement; work done by resistance is negative.
