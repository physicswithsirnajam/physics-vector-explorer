2 · Apply a turning force

A solid uniform disk rotates about its fixed central axis. Positive tangential force at the right edge acts upward, producing counterclockwise torque.

A positive tangential force produces positive angular acceleration; a negative force produces negative angular acceleration.

Experiment controls

A signed force at the rim produces signed torque and angular acceleration.

Clockwise torqueCounterclockwise torque
τ = Fr  ·  I = ½MR²  ·  α = τ/I
Initial angular speed: ω₀ = +2 rad/s. With constant applied torque, ω = ω₀ + αt and θ = ω₀t + ½αt². The model does not include friction.

Torque and angular momentum

The net external torque changes angular momentum: τ = ΔL/Δt. Watch how the signed change develops over time.

For constant torque, angular momentum changes linearly with time.

3 · Same mass, different distribution

Choose a shape. The same mass and radius do not guarantee the same moment of inertia.

The comparison assumes uniform mass distribution for each ideal shape and rotation about the indicated symmetry axis through the centre.

Compare responses to the same torque

The external torque is fixed at +8 N·m for every shape. Only the geometry and resulting inertia change.

Idisk = ½MR²
What changes? At a fixed positive torque, the shape with the smallest moment of inertia has the largest angular acceleration. A thin hoop stores more rotational inertia at the same mass and radius than a uniform solid disk.
