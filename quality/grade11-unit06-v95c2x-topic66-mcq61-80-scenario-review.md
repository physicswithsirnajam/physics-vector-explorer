### Independently checked physics and formative-versus-summative distinctions

**Q61–64 — atomizer/paint sprayer/filter pump:** An atomizer aspirates liquid because local fast crossflow produces lower static pressure near the pickup opening than at the liquid surface, original Q61 A. More airflow can increase entrainment and breakup (Q62 C), but **droplet formation also involves shear and fluid material properties**, not pressure difference alone. The original water-jet filter pump removes air from the connected vessel (Q63 A) and blocking its suction port reduces removal (Q64 A). **Q63 device naming and Q61 author's Understanding description are not automatic Summative/Application B44 certifications.**

**Q65–68 — Torricelli discharge in an open tank:** Ideal `v=sqrt(2gh)`: deeper hole -> greater speed (Q65 A), doubling h -> `sqrt(2)` increase (Q66 D). Density cancels in an open-surface same-fluid ideal comparison (Q67 A); it may influence real losses if different liquids have different viscosities. Falling free-surface head makes instantaneous jet speed decrease (Q68 B) under a quasi-steady draining approximation.

**Q69–73 — pipe constriction, Venturi/piezometer/carburetor:** Steady incompressible lossless horizontal flow through a narrower section has greater average speed and lower static pressure (Q69 D). At equivalent tap elevations and common piezometer fluid, the taller liquid column measures greater static pressure (Q70 A); the original prepared author Understanding level needs individual B44 Application review. For unchanged ideal measured pressure difference and meter geometry, lower density implies greater inferred speed approximately `v∝rho^(-1/2)` (Q71 A). Carburetor fuel aspiration follows low static pressure at the fast-air throat (Q72 D). **Q73** smaller area -> greater section speed independently of labels (C), but that is **continuity B36**, not itself summative B40 pressure application, despite prepared source annotation B40.

**Q74–75 — wing lift and equal transit misconception:** Upper wing pressure 97kPa and lower 100kPa over 12m² yields pressure-force contribution `(100−97)×1000×12=36000N upward` (Q74 A), assuming effective area and roughly representative pressure difference. Q75 A correctly rejects **equal transit time** as an aerodynamic law. True wing lift uses full pressure/shear distribution and momentum deflection, not the simplistic claim that two parcels must meet again simultaneously.

**Q76–77 — Magnus force:** Reversing the sense of spin can reverse lateral flow/pressure asymmetry and associated transverse aerodynamic force (Q76 B, Q77 C), given comparable trajectories and environment. The source author labels both as Understanding; they should not be automatically promoted to official B44 Application without teacher review.

**Q78 — sheets and formative B39:** Original NBF p101 sheet experiment: fast airflow between sheets lowers static pressure and outer pressure pushes them inward, Q78 D. This is a valuable **formative Bernoulli-effect** demonstration (B39), not automatically summative B44 real-world Application.

**Q79 — vertical pipe:** For an ideal single streamline with constant density, when elevation decreases and speed also decreases, both terms' reduction permits increased static pressure at the lower point, Q79 C. No unique numeric pressure is inferred.

**Q80 — printed NBF Assignment6.4 high-speed caveat:** Original printed Assignment6.4 specifies **450m/s above and 410m/s below** a wing. Source D is the best supplied explanation of using a **simplified** Bernoulli model at comparable heights; it is **not sufficient by itself** to establish a physically realistic pressure/lift inference. Those speeds exceed ordinary near-room-temperature air sound speed (~340m/s), introducing compressibility and potential shocks. Also upper and lower local streamlines must be related through a justified upstream total head or reference model; comparing speeds alone does not prove pressure difference. These scientific limitations require teacher disclosure before final examination use. Prior merged PR #128 retained NBF's intended ideal-number outputs **22,188Pa**, **45m²**, **998,460N**, correctly distinguished from real-world validation.
