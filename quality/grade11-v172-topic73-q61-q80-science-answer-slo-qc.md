### Independent numeric/physics spot checks

- **Q63:** for linearly elastic `U∝x²`, total at twice extension is fourfold, while *additional* energy beyond the first state is three times the original. Original keyed distinction is correct.
- **Q70–71:** a correctly labelled force-vs-extension gradient has stiffness N/m; steeper force graph (identical axes and scales) means greater `k`. It is distinct from area/work.
- **Q72:** converting a measured mass/extension gradient from kg/m by multiplication with `g` gives the required N/m.
- **Q74–75:** `U=½Fx` for linear force–extension and `U=½kx²` for Hooke-law springs give the original comparative answers.
- **Q77:** a specified *triangular* graph area yields the correct work; **Q78** correctly calculates ½Fx **in its source context**, but requires an individual linear condition when scored standalone.
- **Q79:** at constant elastic stress in the linear regime, `U/V=σ²/(2Y)` indeed halves when Y doubles; this is an energy-*density* extension, not a wrong answer.
