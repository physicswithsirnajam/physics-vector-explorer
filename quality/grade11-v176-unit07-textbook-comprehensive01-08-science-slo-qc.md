| **7.6** | **Compulsory — textbook graph-axis correction and general integration** | Mechanical work is `W=∫F(x)dx`. In the original **NBF p.122 Fig.7.14, weight is horizontal and extension vertical**: the plotted area represents `∫x dF`, which is **not generally equal** to `∫F dx`. ### Three diagram/assumption guardrails

1. **Ductile stress–strain (Fig.7.12):** stress vertical, strain horizontal; proportional limit, elastic limit/yield and permanent set are distinct physical concepts.
2. **Force–extension (Fig.7.14):** distinguish `∫F dx` from `∫x dF`; an arbitrary curve's work is not the textbook's reversed-axis "area under curve." For the *linear* case alone, work is half the force–extension rectangle.
3. **Young modulus experiment (Fig.7.13):** measure force, original length, area and extension; describe both test and reference wires and appropriate proportional loading. The experiment is a **B-25 Understanding** outcome, while independently applying modulus formulae may be **B-24 Application**.

### Overlap and presentation quality

**Q7.4/Q7.5** form one strong stress–strain-curve overlap group: keep both for learning but do not count them as separate distinct SLOs merely because both require similar diagrams. **Q7.6/Q7.7** also share a force-extension basis but have meaningfully different required outcomes: **why graph area equals mechanical work** versus **deriving the linear-elastic stored-energy relation**. Both can be retained if the paper differentiates those demands.

Check all future authorized model-answer displays in **desktop/projector, mobile and print/PDF**: labelled axes, scientific symbols, `ΔL`, `F`, `Y`, `A`, superscripts, integrals and diagram captions should render, not convert to boxes.
