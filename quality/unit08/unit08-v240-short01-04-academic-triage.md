## Changes or qualifications needing attention

| Original short question | Compulsory or optional? | Scientific rationale | Keep original / add separate item? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8.1 — tyre air pressure after driving** | **OPTIONAL explanatory qualification** | Air in the tyres commonly warms as driving deforms the tyre and dissipates mechanical energy; at approximately fixed gas volume, absolute pressure rises with absolute temperature. A pressure increase is **not guaranteed** regardless of road, ambient conditions, initial inflation and heat loss. For numerical calculations distinguish **absolute** pressure from gauge pressure. | **Keep the original**. A brief teacher note suffices; no new question necessary. |
| **8.2 — internal energy rise at constant temperature** | **OPTIONAL ideal-gas contrast; no original amendment required** | During isothermal melting or vaporisation, latent-heat absorption can raise internal energy through intermolecular potential energy while temperature is fixed. For a fixed amount of **ideal gas**, however, internal energy depends only on temperature, so this phase-change example must not be indiscriminately applied to ideal-gas behaviour. | **Keep the original**. Optional contrast is suitable for the teacher explanation; no replacement needed. |
| **8.3 — isolated system and changing temperature** | **COMPULSORY nuanced answer before assessed use** | An isolated system exchanges neither matter nor energy with its surroundings. Conservation of the **whole system's total energy** does **not** require every internal temperature to remain constant: internal mechanical energy can dissipate, chemical/phase transitions can occur, and energy can move between internal parts. Distinguish an evolving system from a uniform equilibrium state. | **Keep the original** and prepare a conditional, verified marking explanation. Optionally **add a separately authored, well-bounded example** to test the distinction; do not silently rewrite source wording. |
| **8.4 — increase in internal energy from work or heat** | **COMPULSORY precise transfer/path explanation before assessed use** | A final increase in **state function** \(U\) alone does not identify whether the energy entered by a **temperature-difference heat transfer** or by **mechanical/electrical work**, or both. \(Q\) and \(W\) are **process/boundary transfers**, not separately stored energy types. Identify the physical process and use a stated sign convention, e.g. \(\Delta U = Q-W_{\rm by}\). | **Keep the original** and qualify its introductory microscopic/macroscopic analogy rather than replacing it. No new question needed unless a teacher requests a practical scenario. |
