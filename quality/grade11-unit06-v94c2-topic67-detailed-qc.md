## Part B: academic check of all 48 prepared questions

**Q1–Q8 — central concepts:** Prepared answers correctly distinguish zero viscosity from zero **mass**, **density** or **gravity** and link suppressed *viscous* resistance to long-lived circulation. Superfluid phenomena are quantum in character. Any phrase “zero friction” should mean *absence of ordinary viscous friction under idealized conditions*, **not** absence of every possible energy transfer or interaction in actual experimental helium.

**Q9–Q16 — helium and textbook-reported temperature benchmarks:** The owner-prepared source attributes **He-4 below approximately 2.17 K** and **He-3 below approximately 0.0025 K** to the original textbook's information box. These values are internally coherent and `0.0025 K = 2.5 mK`. The source itself warns that the quoted thresholds are not universal across all pressures and materials. Original printed p.106 evidence is still required if making a definitive exact-source claim. The compression/expansion-nozzle narrative is **an introductory account**, not a complete, universally applicable refrigeration process or laboratory instruction.

**Q17–Q24 — superfluid film creep (Fig6.16):** A **thin liquid film** can move over the rim and transfer liquid outward under suitable conditions; this does **not** mean an entire bulk liquid spontaneously leaps over walls or instantaneously empties a sealed container. The absence of viscous dissipation does not explain all surface-film mechanisms by itself; retain the phenomenological textbook explanation. The supplied first diagram is source-described independent art, while the original printed Fig6.16 itself remains unverified here.

**Q25–Q32 — persistent and quantized vortices:** Distinguish qualitative critical **angular velocity** *for a given arrangement* from a universal numerical threshold. Quantization refers to allowed **circulation** units, not that all points in the superfluid move at one of a few allowed linear/angular speeds. Below threshold, the superfluid component can remain vortex-free; above threshold discrete vortices can appear and multiply. The source provides no formula for critical speed/vortex energy, so **do not invent one as prescribed FBISE B43 mathematics**.

**Q33–Q40 — helium phase diagram (Fig6.17):** Source describes **temperature (K) horizontally, pressure (MPa) vertically** and regions **solid**, **normal liquid**, **superfluid** and **gas**. Both coordinates matter in identifying regions. This is **conceptual graph reading**, not a license to infer numerical phase boundaries or high-precision critical pressures from a low-resolution textbook illustration. Source-supplied Figure 6.17 is **reproduced NBF**: cannot be shipped from the private notes to a public student portal. Q40 conversion `0.0025 K×1000=2.5 mK` is correct **supporting arithmetic**, not a newly mandated B43 mathematical SLO.

**Q41–Q48 — applications and exam synthesis:** The source names high-precision gyroscopes, slow-light experiments and neutron-star superfluid possibilities, and distinguishes superconductivity of solids from helium superfluidity. Keep these as **textbook-attributed conceptual examples** with qualifiers; avoid claiming slow-light behaviour is universal to all superfluids or that all neutron-star matter has been directly observed as superfluid. Q45 correctly rejects zero-gravity inference; Q47 correctly recognizes that the official outcome is **Understanding** rather than a vortex-energy calculation.

**Arithmetic finding:** No independently recalculated number in Part B requires a replacement under its stated source givens. The dominant required corrections are *model/source/artwork presentation qualifications*.
