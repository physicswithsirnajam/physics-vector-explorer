**Q54:** The source explicitly selects a simplified formula neglecting buoyancy and uses `r=0.10 mm=10⁻⁴ m`. In a more complete Stokes terminal-speed model, the density factor is `ρ_s−ρ_f`; treating `ρ_s` alone is an approximation, not a general prediction.

**Q59:** The teacher source states that NBF Assignment6.2 specifies raindrop `r=0.3 mm`, `η_air=1.8×10⁻⁵ Pa·s`. Its original-print wording and data remain unverified. The question's chosen answer **C** is still correct **for the explicit formula**, because droplet density `ρ` is required before a numerical value can be determined.

### Optional notes and domain limits

- Q42, Q44–46, Q55–58: the source's linear `F_d∝ηrv` and square `v_t∝r²` predictions hold under the stipulated Stokes model and fixed factors, not in arbitrary flows at higher Reynolds number.
- Q47–49: inversion of Stokes' formula correctly gives `η=0.001 Pa·s`, `r=0.002 m=2 mm`, and `v=0.5 m/s`; input values alone do not establish physical flow regime validity.
- Q50: `[η]=[M L⁻¹ T⁻¹]` from `[F]/([r][v])`. Q51–53: `mPa·s=10⁻³ Pa·s`; honey viscosity varies considerably with composition and temperature.
- Q60: mass uses the **DROPLET**, rather than surrounding fluid, density and `(4/3)πr³`; 1.0 mm must be converted to 0.001 m.
