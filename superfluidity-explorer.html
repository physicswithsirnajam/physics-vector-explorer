Prediction to test

When helium-4 is cooled below about 2.17 K under the usual conditions, what changes about its flow behaviour?

Ordinary liquid: viscous flow dissipates motion.
Superfluid component: persistent flow is possible without ordinary viscous damping.

The exact transition temperature depends on pressure. This module uses the familiar ≈2.17 K helium-4 value near saturated-vapor-pressure conditions.

Important interpretation

The striking wall-film behaviour does not mean liquid creates energy or can climb any wall forever. Conditions and energy differences determine actual film motion. The lesson is that ordinary viscous resistance is dramatically different in the superfluid state.

Teacher question

Does superfluidity mean that a real material has no interactions, no normal component, and no energy losses under all circumstances? Explain.

Discuss before revealing the classroom explanation.
No. Helium-II can contain both normal and superfluid components, and real flows can exhibit vortices and dissipative processes. The diagrams illustrate idealized concepts, not every possible flow situation.
