First Law of Thermodynamics

Follow the energy: heat transferred into a system may increase its internal energy, supply work done by the system, or both. The signs matter as much as the numbers.

8.4 · Energy is conserved

Use the system as the reference. The textbook writes the First Law of Thermodynamics as heat supplied to the system, internal-energy change and work done by the system:

Q = ΔU + WbyEquivalent: ΔU = Q − Wby = Q + Won
QuantityPositive signNegative sign
Heat QEnergy enters the system as heat.Energy leaves the system as heat.
Work WbySystem does work on its surroundings (e.g., gas expansion).Surroundings do net work on the system (e.g., gas compression or stirring).
Internal-energy change ΔUInternal energy increases.Internal energy decreases.
Sign convention: Won = −Wby for the same work transfer. If a question says 25 kJ of work is done on the system, use Won = +25 kJ, or equivalently Wby = −25 kJ. Never treat both as +25 kJ in the same equation. Heat and work are energy transfers; internal energy is a property of the state.

Source: original NBF printed p.135, textbook equation 8.7. Signs throughout this lesson are consistent with Q = ΔU + Wby.

The four special thermodynamic processes

Each process keeps a different quantity fixed or imposes a different energy-transfer condition. Statements about temperature or internal energy below use the ideal-gas model where specified.

8.4.1 · Isothermal

Temperature constant: ΔT = 0. For a fixed amount of ideal gas, internal energy depends only on temperature, so ΔU = 0.

ΔU = 0Ideal gas, constant temperature
Q = Wby

For isothermal ideal-gas expansion, heat entering the system equals work done by the gas. Compression reverses the signs. A constant-temperature curve on a p–V plot is an isotherm.

Original pp.136–137, Figure 8.10 (not copied).

8.4.2 · Adiabatic

No heat transfer: Q = 0. The system may still do work. Expansion lowers the internal energy of a fixed ideal gas; compression raises it.

Q = 0No heat enters or leaves
ΔU = −Wby

Adiabatic does not mean constant temperature. Ideal-gas expansion doing positive work cools the gas when no heat enters.

Original pp.137–138, Figure 8.11 (not copied).

8.4.3 · Isochoric

Volume constant: ΔV = 0. With only pressure–volume boundary work, there is no expansion/compression work.

Wby = 0Fixed-volume boundary; no other work modes
Q = ΔU

Heating a fixed-volume ideal-gas sample generally raises its temperature and pressure. Its p–V path is vertical.

Original printed p.138, Figure 8.12 (not copied).

8.4.4 · Isobaric

Pressure constant: p is unchanged through the process. The gas may expand or compress, performing pressure–volume work.

Wby = p(Vf − Vi)Constant external pressure; p in Pa, V in m³
Q = ΔU + pΔV

A constant-pressure p–V path is horizontal. In expansion, work done by the gas is positive; during compression, it is negative.

Original pp.138–139, Figure 8.13 (not copied).

Distinguish conditions: Isothermal fixes temperature; adiabatic fixes heat transfer at zero; isochoric fixes volume; isobaric fixes pressure. An isothermal ideal-gas process can involve heat and work even though ΔU = 0. An adiabatic process can change temperature even though Q = 0.

Original NBF Example 8.3 · Independent worked adaptation

Source data from printed pp.139–140. The following explanation is independently written, not quoted publisher marking material.

Given: The internal energy of a gas decreases by 200 J, while the gas does 50 J of work on its surroundings. Find: whether heat enters or leaves, and by how much.
Reveal signed steps for Example 8.3
  1. Internal energy decreases: ΔU = −200 J.
  2. Work is done by the gas: Wby = +50 J.
  3. Apply the textbook convention: Q = ΔU + Wby.
  4. Substitute: Q = −200 J + 50 J = −150 J.

Answer: 150 J of heat leaves the gas. The negative Q indicates heat transfer out of the system.

Original NBF Assignment 8.3 · Tea and stirring

Source data from printed p.140. Independently solved with signs shown for each energy transfer.

Given: A cup of tea initially has internal energy Ui = 200 kJ. As it cools, it loses 150 kJ as heat. Stirring does 25 kJ of work on the tea. Find: the internal-energy change and final internal energy.
Reveal independent solution to Assignment 8.3
  1. Heat is lost: Q = −150 kJ.
  2. Work done on the tea: Won = +25 kJ; in the textbook's work-by convention, Wby = −25 kJ.
  3. Use ΔU = Q − Wby = −150 kJ − (−25 kJ) = −125 kJ.
  4. Compute Uf = Ui + ΔU = 200 kJ − 125 kJ = 75 kJ.

Answer: ΔU = −125 kJ; Uf = 75 kJ. The stirring returns some energy to the tea, but heat loss is larger.

Four concept checks · Answers revealed on demand

1. Can heat enter a gas without increasing its internal energy?
Yes. For isothermal expansion of a fixed ideal gas, ΔU = 0 and the incoming heat is converted into work done by the gas: Q = Wby.
2. An adiabatic ideal gas expands while doing +300 J of work. What is ΔU?
Q = 0; ΔU = −Wby = −300 J. Its internal energy decreases.
3. A rigid closed vessel receives 500 J of heat and does no other work. How much does U change?
At constant volume without other work, Wby = 0, so ΔU = Q = +500 J.
4. A gas is compressed. Should its work-by-system value be positive or negative?
Negative for compression against a positive external pressure, because ΔV < 0 and Wby = pΔV < 0. Work done on it is positive.
